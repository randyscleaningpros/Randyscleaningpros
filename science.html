🔬 The Science Behind The Shine

Why Our Gold Standard Process Actually Works

It's not magic — it's chemistry, physics, and the right order of operations. Here's exactly why each part of our method gets better results.

🧪

Surface Identification: Chemistry Compatibility ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Every surface has a different chemical makeup — natural stone is porous and reacts to acids, sealed wood has a finish that can be stripped by the wrong solvent, stainless steel scratches and streaks with abrasives. By identifying the surface before we apply anything, we match the product's pH and formula to what won't damage it. That's the difference between cleaning and accidentally etching your countertop.

🌀

Dust Removal: Capture vs. Redistribute ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Dust is mostly skin cells, fabric fibers, and pollen — light enough to become airborne with the slightest disturbance. Wiping it with a dry or wet cloth just launches it into the air, where it resettles minutes later. A HEPA-filtered vacuum instead captures particles at the source, pulling them out of your air entirely instead of just moving them around the room.

⏱️

Dwell Time: Letting Surfactants Do the Work ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Degreasers and disinfectants work through surfactant molecules that surround and lift grease, or active ingredients that need contact time to kill germs. Spray-and-immediately-wipe barely scratches the surface — literally. By letting our solution sit for a few minutes, the chemistry has time to fully break down buildup on its own, which means less scrubbing for us and a gentler process for your surfaces.

🔝

Top-to-Bottom, Dry-to-Wet Sequencing ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Gravity doesn't care about your schedule — dust and debris fall down. That's why we always dust ceiling fans and shelves before we vacuum the floor, and clean counters before mopping. We also do dry tasks (dusting, vacuuming) before wet tasks (spraying, mopping), so we're never re-contaminating a surface we already finished.

🦠

Disinfection Contact Time ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

A disinfectant only kills what it touches for as long as label directions require — usually a few minutes of "wet contact time." Wiping it off too fast means it never actually disinfected anything, it just moved germs around. We time this step so every high-touch surface gets the full contact time it needs to actually work.

✨

Polish & Seal: Protecting What We Just Cleaned ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

The final polish isn't just for shine — a light protective layer on stainless steel or a wood conditioner on finished wood helps repel fingerprints, dust, and moisture until your next cleaning. It's the difference between a clean that looks good for a day and one that holds up for weeks.
